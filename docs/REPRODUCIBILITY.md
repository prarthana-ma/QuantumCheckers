## 3. Reproduction boundary (brief §10, capped at ~25-30% of effort)

**Archived research path:** the reported batches reproduce only with the legacy
`DeterministicToyEngine`, a 4×4 probability-only model. The historical experiment's
*shape* was a checkers variant with piece superposition over squares, chance-weighted
occupancy, collapses resolved by the engine's chance rule, and a toy-specific additive
stage structure. Our
`qcheckers/engine/toy.py` is an independent implementation of that model in probabilities
(**not** amplitudes), written from the paper's rules section.

**Not reproduced:** the upstream Cheqqers engine and its published numerical results.
The local `PaperQuantumEngine` now stores complex amplitudes and phases, but it is an
independent implementation with explicit completion and promotion-path choices; it is
not the authors' code and was not used for the archived study. Consequently the archived
numbers are **not** a reproduction of any figure in the paper.

**Added on separate paths:** the archived toy study had a voluntary `observe` action,
legacy probability-aware agent and matched Observe toggle. That agent also altered
normalized-progress scoring and rollout sampling, so the full baseline-to-agent
comparison was not uncertainty-only. The local paper engine implements Levels 0–3 and
adds custom Level 4 decoherence/Observe. These two paths have separate results and
conformance boundaries.

## 5. Ablation definitions, sample sizes, results

Two conditions, matched seeds (0..169), matched opponent, single variable:

| | condition A | condition B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| label | `ablation-prob-vs-random-obs` | `ablation-prob-vs-random-noobs` |
| player 0 | `prob` (probability-aware search) | same agent, same seed |
| player 1 | `random` | same |
| engine level | 2 | 2 |
| `allow_observe` | **True** | **False** |
| games | 170 | 170 |

```
python scripts/run_ablation.py --agent-a prob --agent-b random -n 170 --write-logs
```

| metric | A (observe on) | B (observe off) | difference (A−B) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| p0 win rate | 0.971 [0.933, 0.987] | 0.976 [0.941, 0.991] | −0.006 [−0.046, +0.033] |
| p1 win rate | 0.029 [0.013, 0.067] | 0.024 [0.009, 0.059] | +0.006 [−0.033, +0.046] |
| draw rate | 0.000 [0.000, 0.022] | 0.000 [0.000, 0.022] | +0.000 [−0.022, +0.022] |
| collapse rate | 0.700 [0.627, 0.764] | 0.000 [0.000, 0.022] | +0.700 [+0.624, +0.764] |
| mean length | 12.63 plies | 12.62 plies | — |
| observe moves/game | 1.16 | 0.00 | — |

**Reading, stated plainly:** the only difference whose interval excludes zero is the
collapse rate, which is true by construction — switching voluntary measurement on
produces measurements. The win-rate difference **includes zero**: at this sample size, on
this board, with this search budget, voluntary measurement did **not** measurably change
who won. That is a null result on the effect we care about, and it is reported as one
rather than tuned away (brief §12: a negative outcome is acceptable and more credible).

**Baseline conditions** (`run_baseline.py`, n=170 each, level 0 and 2):

| condition | p0 win [95% Wilson] | collapse rate | mean length | games/s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `classical-random-vs-random` | 0.218 [0.162, 0.286] | 0.000 | 7.90 | 1984 |
| `classical-mcts-vs-random` | 0.547 [0.472, 0.620] | 0.000 | 8.42 | 56 |
| `quantum-observe-vs-random` | 0.176 [0.127, 0.241] | 0.212 | 8.66 | 1477 |
| `quantum-prob-vs-random` | 0.976 [0.941, 0.991] | 0.000 | 12.62 | 13.8 |

Two honest observations from this table:

* **A first-player disadvantage at level 0** (0.218 wins for the side to move). This is a
  property of *our* 4×4 engine, not of the paper's game, and it makes every other level-0
  comparison a comparison against that baseline rather than against 0.5.
* **The observe agent loses to random** (0.176). An agent that spends whole turns
  resolving its own pieces without a reason to is spending tempo; this is the mechanical
  explanation, and it is the opposite of a claim that measurement helps.

## 6. Logs and plots regenerate from seeds — verified, not asserted

```
python scripts/verify_results.py --json results/verification.json
```

The verifier rebuilds the engine, both agents, the board size and the ply cap from **the
log line alone**, replays the game, and requires the regenerated line to be byte-identical:

```
1025 records in 8 logs: 1020/1020 experiment records regenerated byte-for-byte,
5 game-client records checked for level only
```

The five game-client records are server rooms where people played seats; human moves are
not seed-regenerable, so those records are level-checked and never replayed.
The verifier refuses (exit 1) a record at a non-batchable level, a record that does not
reproduce, and a malformed line. Writing it found a real defect: the ply cap is a
*terminal condition* the search reads, so a log that omits it cannot be regenerated in
general. `max_plies` is now part of the record (`docs/PROTOCOL.md` §4), and the verifier is
covered by seven tests of its own.

```
python scripts/make_plots.py     -> results/plots/*.png   (4 figures, each stating its n)
```

## 7. Archived toy-engine mismatches with the paper

These comparisons describe the archived 4×4 `DeterministicToyEngine` only, not the
current local `PaperQuantumEngine`. For the current implementation's paper-level mapping
and explicit modeling choices, see [`RULE_CONFORMANCE.md`](RULE_CONFORMANCE.md). The
toy-engine differences below were found by reading the paper against
`qcheckers/engine/toy.py`.

| # | Paper (§III) | Our engine | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Captures are obligatory** when one is available | captures are optional | our branching factor is larger and our play is weaker than the paper's; level-0 win rates are not comparable to theirs |
| 2 | **Crowning**: a piece reaching the far rank becomes a king and moves backwards | no crowning at all | endgames differ; a piece can never come back |
| 3 | **Draw at 40 moves without a capture** | no draw rule; games end only by `no_pieces`, `no_legal_moves` or our ply cap | every draw rate here is a structural zero, and a ply cap is a *punishment* for long games rather than a rule of the game |
| 4 | Level 1 = superposition **and measurement**; level 2 = **entanglement**; level 3 = **interference**, and measurement is triggered only by capture | level 1 = spread, level 2 = *voluntary* resolution, level 3 = merge, level 4 = decoherence | our "levels" are stages of *our* mechanic ladder; they must never be read as the paper's levels, and the client labels them **stages** for that reason |
| 5 | A **split piece part can move as a classical piece**, and a split piece can split again | a spread piece is immobile until resolved | the central tactical tension is different: ours punishes splitting, theirs rewards it |
| 6 | The **merge** joins two parts *of the same original piece* | our merge joins two *different* pieces | our level 3 is a different move that happens to share a name |
| 7 | Real **amplitudes and phases** via `cirq`; interference cannot be simulated probabilistically | classical probabilities only, no phase | our level 3 cannot reproduce interference at all; every "quantum" claim here is about chances |
| 8 | 8×8 English draughts, one row of pieces for the small-board experiments, 5×5 and 8×8 results | 4×4, two rows | nothing here is the same game size as any reported figure |
| 9 | MCTS: **200/400/800 rollouts, c = 2√2** | 24 UCT iterations, c = √2 | our baseline is a much weaker player; at this budget it only narrowly beats random (0.547 [0.472, 0.620]), which is *not* evidence against their result at 800 rollouts |

These differences bound claims based on the archived dataset; they are not a current
implementation checklist. The paper engine implements many of these paper rules, but it
is still an independent implementation with documented choices and limits.

## 8. Novelty and collision risk

* **Voluntary measurement** — not present in the paper (measurement there is triggered by
  capture attempts only). Our `observe` action and its ablation arm are an addition.
* **Probability-aware averaging** — the paper's §VII states the open problem in its own
  words (§1 above), and the paper's own suggestion is about node selection and heuristics;
  our change is in the rollout's action sampling and the evaluation. **Novel relative to
  the paper**, but see the caveat below.
* **Search for follow-up work** — targeted queries only (title, "voluntary measurement"
  in quantum games, probability-weighted MCTS averaging, tournament/follow-up reports).
  None of the hits studied this game's measurement as a voluntary action or weighted its
  rollouts by occupancy chance. **Caveat: this is a narrow search, not a systematic
  review.** Until a systematic one is done, the novelty claim in `TEAM.md` stays in its
  "pending" form, and nothing in this repository should be published as a new contribution.
* **Licence (real constraint)** — the upstream Cheqqers repository is **AGPL-3.0** (read
  directly from its README). Strong copyleft, so copying, modifying or hosting their code
  would carry source-sharing obligations. We copied none of it: the toy engine, agents,
  runner, server and client in this repository are our own, written against the paper's
  rules section and against our own protocol document.

## 9. Limitations, and what is still unverified

1. **The upstream engine is not integrated** (Gate 2's tag was read, not run). Everything
   measured here is the toy engine, on a 4×4 board with two rows of pieces. The current
   local paper engine is separate and is not evaluated by these experiments.
2. **The archived toy engine has no amplitudes, phases, entanglement or interference.**
   The local paper engine does retain complex amplitudes and implements paper-based
   entanglement/interference rules; see [`RULE_CONFORMANCE.md`](RULE_CONFORMANCE.md).
3. **Our MCTS is not the paper's MCTS** (mismatch 9). No comparison with their published
   win rates is made or implied.
4. **The ablation's win-rate difference includes zero.** On this engine, at this sample
   size and budget, the effect of voluntary measurement on the winner was not detectable.
   The collapse-rate difference is mechanical, not evidence of skill.
5. **Every archived draw rate is a structural zero**, because the toy engine's 40-move
   rule was not implemented (mismatch 3). The local paper engine implements the rule.
6. **Archived Levels 3 and 4 are unevaluated.** The toy batch runner refuses those
   levels. The current paper engine offers paper Level 3 in live play and adds custom
   Level 4; neither is covered by the archived experiment results.
7. **The follow-up search is narrow** (§8), so the novelty claim stays pending.
8. **The demo's third seed is a single game.** `demo-decoherence.json` is a level-4 game
   played with `play()` directly, because a level-4 *batch* is forbidden by design; no
   aggregate claim is made about level 4 anywhere.
9. **Time cost:** the whole pilot — 1020 logged games, plots, verification, browser
   acceptance — runs in a few minutes on a laptop. The long poles are the probability-aware
   games (13.8/s) and the Playwright check (~3 min), not the statistics.
