# Paper-rule conformance and implementation boundary

This note maps the local `PaperQuantumEngine` to the staged rules in Raat et al.,
[*Quantum Checkers*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.05962). It describes the implementation
in this repository; it does not claim that the upstream Cheqqers code was integrated
or that archived experiment results were generated by this engine.

The reference game uses English draughts, with levels 0–3 adding superposition and
measurement, entanglement, then interference. The local paper engine represents those
mechanics with a CPU-side sparse complex-amplitude state. Level 4 is a project extension.

## Levels

| Level | Paper rule | Local `PaperQuantumEngine` behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Classical English draughts. | Ordinary moves, compulsory captures and capture chains, crowning, no pieces/no legal moves endings, and the 40 quiet-move draw rule. |
| 1 | Superposition and measurement. Split pieces can move and split again. Captures measure the relevant split piece(s), following the paper's order; a failed location attempt passes the turn. | Split actions apply a complex gate to the local modes. Capture resolution samples the required location(s) with the seeded local sampler, or an injected hardware sampler when configured. |
| 2 | Adds entanglement. An unsplit attacker attempting to capture a split defender creates correlated capture/no-move branches rather than first measuring the defender. | The complete board configurations remain joint branches, so the attacker and defender are correlated in the complex state. |
| 3 | Adds interference. Two parts of the same original piece that can reach a common square may be merged by the inverse split operation; phase can leave amplitude on source squares. | A merge applies the adjoint of the local split matrix after the ordinary-move phases. Interference changes the resulting amplitudes and displayed occupancy marginals. |
| 4 | No Level 4 in the paper. | Custom project rules add a coherence timer and voluntary `Observe`; `allow_observe` is true only at this level in `PaperQuantumEngine`. |

Paper levels 0–3 are the source of the local engine's rule design. The engine is an
independent implementation, not the authors' code or a claim of bit-for-bit agreement
with their implementation. Section III and the Appendix of the paper are the primary
rule references; the Appendix gives the split amplitudes and identifies merge as the
inverse split operation.

## Exact-state representation and choices

### Empty destinations and measurement

Ordinary moves, capture landings, and both split destinations must be clear of every
piece's possible positions, including the acting piece's own other branches: a square
the same piece could occupy in another branch is not an empty destination, even if it
is empty in some complete-board branches. Merge destinations are the one exception:
the acting piece itself may be possibly present, since joining its own branches is
what a merge does, but every other piece must still be clear. Such actions are rejected before applying
the move, without measuring or collapsing either piece. Blocked capture landings
are excluded before compulsory-capture priority is evaluated.

A selected part of a split piece can still move to a clear square while its other
parts remain in place. At paper Level 1, capture attempts trigger the measurements
specified in Section III-B; ordinary moves and splits do not. Level 2 additionally
supports the paper's coherent capture/no-capture operation. Level 4's voluntary
Observe and timer remain explicit project extensions.

The engine stores a sparse mapping from complete labelled board configurations to
complex amplitudes. A configuration includes each piece's square or absence and its
king status. Actions transform these branches on the CPU; probabilities shown to the
client are derived marginals (`|amplitude|²`). This representation preserves joint
correlations and relative phase that cannot be reconstructed from independent per-piece
probabilities.

The paper gives the split's action on the source basis state:

\[
|100\rangle \mapsto \frac{1+i}{2}|010\rangle + \frac{1-i}{2}|001\rangle.
\]

The local implementation completes that specified source column to a full 3×3 unitary
with an orthonormal extension in [`paper.py`](../qcheckers/engine/paper.py). Merge uses
the adjoint of that chosen matrix. This completion is necessary to define the operator
on every local basis input, but the paper does not state those additional columns as
the unique required completion. Therefore, paths involving inputs outside the stated
source column reflect an explicit local modeling choice and may differ from the
upstream implementation.

Promotion also needs a representation choice when branches with different promotion
histories later meet. The local engine adds a hidden, path-tagged promotion ancilla to
the joint configuration. It is not a board piece and is not exposed in occupancy; it
keeps those histories distinguishable so later transformations do not merge them as
if they were the same path. This is an implementation device, not an additional player
action in the paper.

## CPU simulation, IBM sampling, and resource limits

The local engine does **not** keep a board state on a quantum processor. The full sparse
complex state is represented and evolved in Python on the CPU. If the optional IBM
sampler is enabled, a collapse submits a measurement circuit for the candidate-square
distribution and uses the returned sample to project the CPU-held state. The quantum
processor supplies that measurement sample; it does not hold or evolve the persistent
board state. Archived batch runs used seeded local sampling and no hardware jobs.

The engine defaults to a maximum of **4,096 sparse branches** for one exact operation.
An operation that would exceed the cap raises `BranchLimitError` before returning a
partial state; the engine restores its local RNG state on failure. This bound controls
resource use and can make a legal offered action unavailable to a particular simulation.
Live MCTS skips a capped simulation edge and prefers another simulated root action when
available.

`PaperQuantumEngine` defaults to a **1,000-ply safety limit**, and the production
`create_app()` path explicitly passes the same **1,000 plies** when creating rooms. A
directly constructed `Room` or compatibility `RoomRegistry` still has a 200-ply default;
that is not the production app default. The paper's separate 40-move no-capture draw
rule is also implemented (`forty_move_draw`).

## Live wire view

The production paper-rules server uses an 8×8 board by default (configurable from 4 to
16). Seat 0 is White and moves first; the initial White pieces occupy the far rows at
the bottom of the board. Each completed or continuing capture jump increments `ply`;
a forced capture continuation keeps the same seat to move. The 40-move no-capture draw
is tracked separately from this per-action ply count.

The live `board` identifies itself with `engine: "paper-quantum"` and
`paper_rules: true`. It does not include the archived JSONL field `toy_engine`; that
flag belongs to experiment/replay records, not the live board payload. The board exposes
occupancy marginals only: each piece's `sites` and `king_sites` are weighted square
lists, with `survival_probability` giving the chance that the piece still exists.
`branches` and `branch_count` report the number of exact joint configurations;
`continuation_piece` identifies a forced multi-jump, and `quiet_moves` supports the
40-move draw rule. `coherence_ticks` is keyed by piece id. Full complex amplitudes and
their phases remain server-side and are not displayed or sent in gameplay payloads.

If every offered AI action would exceed the 4,096-branch exact-operation cap, live MCTS
falls back to the first legal action to preserve the engine's legal-action contract.
Applying that action can then be refused by the engine with no state change. `RetryAI`
retries the same position and fixed decision seed, so it cannot make that capped
transition succeed; the player must resign or start a new game. When at least one other
offered action can be simulated, capped alternatives are excluded from the AI choice.

## Which results use which engine

The archived 1,020-game experiment set and its plots were generated by
`DeterministicToyEngine`, the 4×4 probability-only research model. Those records remain
reproducible with their original engine and frozen legacy agents. They are not results
for `PaperQuantumEngine`, and they do not measure the current live MCTS agent.

The local game server now defaults to `PaperQuantumEngine` through
`server.app.create_app(paper_rules=True)`. This source configuration does not prove that
the already hosted deployment runs the same revision. See
[`HANDOFF.md`](HANDOFF.md) for the source/deployment distinction and
[`REPRODUCIBILITY.md`](REPRODUCIBILITY.md) for the archived experiment boundary.
